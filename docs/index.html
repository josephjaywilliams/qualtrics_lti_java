Why one minute

This app follows the four design principles from a 14-day study of one-minute prompts with 22 adults, who reported completing 82.5% of them.

Ultra-brief and immediate

One action you can start right now, with no planning or scheduling.

Low scaffolding

No account and no setup. The timer's guide is optional; the words alone are enough.

Clear endpoint

Sixty seconds, then you're done. You always know when you've finished.

Reward first

Each prompt says what you get out of it, so it's easier to say yes.

The study also found four kinds of friction that stop even a one-minute action: timing, place, missing resources and mental effort. The "anything in the way" and "not now" buttons use those four, so the app stops suggesting what doesn't fit your moment. Rewriting a prompt in your own words is the lightweight personalization the authors recommend.

Based on Hassanzadeh, Hsu, Kornfield, Haag, Bhattacharjee, Olson, Smeddinck, Farb, Mariakakis, Chilton & Williams (2026), Designing for the Moment: How One-Minute Interventions Fit or Falter Across Domains. Prompts marked "From the study" are the paper's own; the rest follow its principles.
